`pyfect.graphql` is a GraphQL client: an operation carries its printed document and the
schemas of its variables and result, and a client sends it through a transport and decodes
the answer. There is no GraphQL parser at runtime. It follows Effect's own
`effect/graphql` client, which does not use graphql-js either.

## Operations and clients

`query(name, document=..., variables=..., result=...)`, `mutation(...)` and
`subscription(...)` declare operations; `variables` is left out for an operation without
any. A subclass of `GraphQLGroup` collects them as attributes, and `client.make(Group)`
gives an instance whose methods are typed by each operation: call `group.repo_stars(
variables)` and get the decoded result. Each call also takes `headers`, `url_params`, a
per-call `context`, and `partial=True`, which gives a `PartialResult` with the `data` and
the `errors` instead of failing on GraphQL errors. A non-2xx HTTP response never succeeds,
even with `partial=True`: it is a `ResponseError` when its body has `errors`, and a
`TransportError` with the status, headers and `Retry-After` otherwise.

A failure is a `GraphQLClientError` naming the operation, with a `reason`:
`ResponseError` (the server's `errors`, the status and headers), `TransportError`,
`EncodeError`, `DecodeError` or `PaginationError`. `is_retryable` holds for a 429, a 5xx
or a valid `Retry-After`, and `retry_after` reads that header.

## Transports

`layer_http(url=...)` sends through the [HTTP client](/docs/platform): POST by default, or
GET for queries with `query_method="GET"`. The URL is a string, an `Effect`, or a function
of the call's context and operation. Subscriptions use graphql-sse over the same URL, or a
graphql-transport-ws connection with `web_socket=protocol.WebSocketOptions(...)`.
`layer_web_socket(options)` runs every operation over one shared graphql-transport-ws
connection, opened on the first call, with `connection_params`, keep-alive pings and an
idle timeout. Both WebSocket transports need a `WebSocketConstructor`, as in Effect: provide
`pyfect.socket.ws.layer_web_socket_constructor` (pyright reports it missing otherwise).
Subscriptions are [streams](/docs/streams), retried on retryable errors by
`client.default_subscription_retry` (or `client.make(Group, subscription_retry=...)`).

## Middleware

A subclass of `GraphQLMiddleware[E, R]` is a service tag. Declare a group as
`GraphQLGroup[Auth]`, or attach one to an operation with `.middleware(Auth)`, and the
client runs the middleware provided for it around each call, group middleware outermost.
A group whose operations bring their own middleware names it as its second type argument,
`GraphQLGroup[Never, Auth]`, so that `client.make` requires it; leaving it out is a
`TypeError` when the class is made.
`map_request(f)` rewrites the request, adding a token for example, and its failures `E`
reach the caller; `Implementation(execute=..., subscribe=...)` wraps the whole call and
sees the transport's status.

## Pagination and batching

`client.pages(group.issues, variables=..., connection=...)` follows a Relay connection's
`endCursor`, sending it as `$after` until `hasNextPage` is false, as a stream of pages;
`client.items` streams their nodes. `pyfect.graphql.resolver` batches lookups like a
[SQL resolver](/docs/sql#a-resolver-that-batches-lookups): `ordered` reads one item per
key by position (`nodes(ids:)`), `find_by_id` matches items by a key, and `aliased`
repeats an operation under aliases, up to 10 per request. `ordered` and `find_by_id` take
`items=lambda data: data["search"]["nodes"]`, which picks the list out of the result and
gives the lookups its item type (Effect's `path=("search", "nodes")` also works, with
lookups typed `Any`). `resolver.request(r)(key)` is one lookup; lookups made together
become one request.

## Testing

`client.make_test(Group, repo_stars=handler)` gives a client whose operations call
handlers instead of a server, and `client.layer_test(Tag, Group, ...)` provides it through
a service tag. An operation without a handler dies with an `UnimplementedError`.
